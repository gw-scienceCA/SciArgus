#Machine Learning and AI
Research on the use of machine learning and AI to advance the measurement and monitoring of biodiversity and conservation efforts. This includes the development of new foundation models across modalities (such as text, remote sensing, and images) with conservation applications, as well as novel benchmarks that measure AI capabilities in this field. Additionally, we welcome thought leadership and perspectives on the promises and perils of integrating AI into conservation practices.

#Remote Sensing of Biodiversity
Research into the application of remote sensing for the quantification of biodiversity and ecosystems at both local and global scales. This encompasses all sensor technologies—including drones, airborne and satellite LiDAR, radar, and optical sensors (particularly hyperspectral). Topics should cover the development of new sensor technologies, the creation of novel algorithms for ecosystem monitoring, and advancements in leveraging existing sensors to derive critical biodiversity information.

#Conservation Science
Thought leadership and perspectives on the current state of conservation science, including evaluations of what is and is not working in global implementation and policy. This section welcomes reflections on the directions taken by conservation actors (governments, NGOs, and private entities), analyses of past successes and failures, and strategic visions for the future of the field.

#Ecological Condition
Papers discussing the concept of ecological condition and its quantification. This includes the introduction of new datasets, critical analyses of existing data, and applications of these datasets to novel questions at global or local scales (provided local datasets are used to generate new, scalable frameworks or approaches). We also welcome discussions on how ecological condition is operationalized and how it feeds into global agreements, such as the Global Biodiversity Framework, or efforts to quantify and commoditize biodiversity.

#Sustainable Groundwater Management Act
Research advancing the science, policy, and implementation of sustainable groundwater management under SGMA and related groundwater governance frameworks. This includes the development of methods for basin planning, sustainable management criteria, groundwater sustainability plan (GSP) evaluation, adaptive management, stakeholder engagement, and decision-support tools that support long-term groundwater sustainability. Additionally, we welcome perspectives on emerging challenges and opportunities for integrating science, policy, and management in groundwater-dependent regions.

#Groundwater Dependent Ecosystems
Research on the identification, characterization, monitoring, and conservation of groundwater-dependent ecosystems (GDEs). This includes studies of ecological responses to groundwater conditions, development of ecological indicators and thresholds, mapping and prioritization approaches, and methods for assessing vulnerability to groundwater depletion and climate change. We also welcome interdisciplinary work that strengthens the integration of ecology and groundwater science to support ecosystem resilience and biodiversity conservation.

#Streamflow Depletion
Research focused on understanding, quantifying, and managing streamflow depletion caused by groundwater pumping and other groundwater stresses. This includes analytical, numerical, and data-driven approaches for assessing groundwater-surface water interactions, estimating depletion impacts, evaluating mitigation strategies, and informing sustainable groundwater management. Additionally, we encourage studies that improve understanding of ecological, hydrologic, and management implications of altered streamflow regimes.

#Managed Aquifer Recharge 
Research advancing the science, design, implementation, and evaluation of managed aquifer recharge (MAR) systems. Topics include recharge site characterization, infiltration processes, water quality outcomes, aquifer storage and recovery, monitoring technologies, and optimization of recharge operations under changing climatic and hydrologic conditions. We also welcome studies exploring the role of MAR in improving water security, enhancing groundwater sustainability, and increasing climate resilience.

#Hydrogeophysics
Research on the application of geophysical methods to characterize, monitor, and understand groundwater systems. This includes advances in airborne, surface, borehole, and satellite-based geophysical techniques, integration of geophysical and hydrologic datasets, and development of novel approaches for imaging subsurface properties and processes. Additionally, we welcome studies demonstrating how hydrogeophysics can improve groundwater management, aquifer characterization, and understanding of groundwater-dependent ecosystems.

#Groundwater Modeling
Research on the development and application of groundwater models to improve understanding and management of subsurface hydrologic systems. This includes numerical, analytical, and hybrid modeling approaches; model calibration and uncertainty analysis; coupled groundwater-surface water simulations; and integration of emerging data sources and machine learning techniques. We also encourage contributions that demonstrate how groundwater modeling can support sustainable water management, ecosystem conservation, and decision-making under uncertainty.
